# Apple design review

## Executive verdict

**Ready with polish.** Discovery Box already feels calm, intentional and unusually truthful for a hackathon demo. At 1440×1000, the hero has an immediate hierarchy: proposition, three market facts, then one clear action. The serif display voice differentiates the product while system UI text keeps controls familiar. The requested buy/open, Ticket, Collectible, attack and maturity interactions all retain user agency and visibly distinguish local simulation from an onchain claim.

There is no release blocker. Three high-leverage issues keep it from being fully ready: the strongest simulation signal disappears from the mobile header, much of the evidence layer is set at 8–10px with weak text-scaling resilience, and a roughly 6,983px desktop / 10,530px mobile story has no in-page wayfinding while two unrelated progress sequences both begin at “01”.

### Review basis

- Live Browser pass at **1440×1000**, **390×844** and **320×700**, covering initial and completed hero states plus Ticket, Collectible, attack and maturity controls.
- Source audit of the rendered components and responsive/accessibility rules for exact evidence.
- “Verified” below means directly observed in the live app and/or mechanically established from the current source. “Taste” is isolated later and is not required for readiness.

## What is already strong

- **Purpose and hierarchy:** At 1440×1000, the hero is calm, premium and immediately scannable. The proposition at `web/App.tsx:282–289`, market facts at `web/App.tsx:291–307`, and primary action at `web/App.tsx:315–348` form a clear claim → evidence → action sequence with no overflow.
- **Typography with a reason:** The system-oriented UI stack at `web/styles.css:1–7` and Iowan/Palatino display stack at `web/styles.css:149–157` create an effective product/editorial contrast. The display serif is distinctive without making controls unfamiliar; it should be retained.
- **Mobile structure:** The 820px and 540px breakpoints at `web/styles.css:523–619` reflow two-column sections, tabs, evidence ledgers and actions cleanly. Live inspection at 390×844 and 320×700 found no horizontal overflow; cards and controls stack in a sensible order.
- **Direct mapping and feedback:** The hero action changes the box, metrics, maturity rail and flow ledger together (`web/App.tsx:224–266`, `web/App.tsx:315–416`). Ticket and Collectible outcomes keep controls adjacent to the artifacts they affect (`web/JudgeMode.tsx:209–290`). Attack selection maps directly to one terminal result (`web/JudgeMode.tsx:372–417`).
- **Direct manipulation:** The maturity slider updates on every `input` event at `web/JudgeMode.tsx:473–484`; the chart and readout track the control continuously rather than waiting for release.
- **Claim responsibility:** Simulation, derived data, tested references, production boundaries and unimplemented modules are separated explicitly throughout `web/App.tsx:108–176` and `web/JudgeMode.tsx:43–150`, `web/JudgeMode.tsx:312–367`, `web/JudgeMode.tsx:372–428`.
- **Motion restraint:** State bridges are short, non-bouncy and limited to meaningful transitions. Reduced motion removes movement while preserving opacity/status feedback at `web/styles.css:621–635`.
- **Materials:** Warm paper surfaces, dark evidence terminals and restrained blue/green/orange accents establish hierarchy without turning every panel into glass. Shadows are consistent enough to read as surface separation rather than decoration.

## Top three highest-leverage changes

1. **Restore “Local demo” to the mobile first viewport.** This is the smallest change with the largest trust benefit.
2. **Raise and rem-base the evidence typography.** The technical proof should be as legible as the product pitch.
3. **Add a compact judge walkthrough and disambiguate the two progress systems.** This turns a collection of good sections into one rehearsable story.

## Deliberate non-recommendations

- **Do not add gratuitous glass.** A sticky translucent toolbar, glass cards inside glass cards, or stronger blur would weaken legibility and add decoration. If wayfinding becomes sticky, prefer a quiet solid or near-solid strip.
- **Do not add springs or bounce to button-driven state swaps.** There is no drag, flick or velocity to justify overshoot. The current critically damped-feeling cubic transitions fit the product.
- **Do not turn applications or attacks into swipeable carousels.** Explicit tabs and buttons are more familiar, more accessible and faster for a judge comparing claims.
- **Do not animate the maturity chart ornamentally.** The slider already provides 1:1 feedback; extra chart travel would make quantitative comparison harder.
- **Do not replace the display serif with an all-system treatment.** Apple-style design is not visual mimicry. The serif earns its place by distinguishing the product narrative from protocol controls.
- **Do not add sound, haptics, confetti or celebratory particles.** This is an evidence-led protocol demo; state, completion and error feedback already carry the necessary meaning.

## Final verdict

**Ready with polish.** The demo is coherent, responsive and trustworthy enough to present now. Addressing the three high-severity findings would materially improve judge comprehension and mobile trust without changing its visual character. The remaining recommendations are targeted accessibility and interaction refinements, not a redesign.
